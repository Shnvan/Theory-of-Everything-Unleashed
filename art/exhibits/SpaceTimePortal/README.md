## Composition

The **standard museum "rubber sheet" analogy**: a horizontal grid mesh deformed downward around a central mass. A small dark mass sphere sits at the bottom of the depression. Two curved marker lines pass on either side of the mass, hinting at bent geodesics.

## What is accurate

- **Central well shape** decreases monotonically with `1/r` — a smooth analogue of gravitational potential wells.
- **Two-sided geodesic bending** shown by marker lines curving toward the mass, corresponding to gravitational lensing of light.
- **Never described as a working portal** — per the accuracy doc's explicit requirement.

## What is interpretation (disclosed)

- **The depression profile is a VISUAL ANALOGY, not the Schwarzschild embedding.** The Flamm paraboloid — the actual isometric embedding of the Schwarzschild spatial slice — has profile `z(r) = 2·sqrt(r_s · (r - r_s))` and diverges at the horizon. This exhibit uses `z(r) = -A/(r + r0)` because the true embedding produces a shape less recognizable as the museum rubber-sheet analogy. **Factual card must state this**.
- **The rubber-sheet analogy itself is a museum convention with known pedagogical shortcomings.** It confuses spatial curvature with the potential well and implicitly requires an "outside" gravitational field to pull the ball into the sheet — a circular explanation. The card should acknowledge this alongside the analogy.
- **Materials.** Brass sheet under D-016. Real museum versions are typically a stretched fabric.
- **The two geodesic marker lines are illustrative, not integrated null geodesics.** They curve toward the mass in the plane of the sheet — real light bending happens in 3D space.
