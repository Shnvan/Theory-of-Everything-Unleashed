## Structure

- Tall pedestal with base and cap (Baroque library-instrument silhouette)
- Circular brass deck at pedestal top
- Central Sun on a small raising post
- **Four planets** on radial arms: **Mercury, Venus, Earth (with its own Moon), Mars**
- Concentric orbital rings on the deck showing the planetary orbits

## What is accurate

- **Four-planet set matches the 1712 design.** Jupiter and Saturn were added to later grand orreries; Uranus wasn't discovered until 1781. Rowley's original included only the visible-to-the-naked-eye inner planets plus Mars.
- **Earth has its own Moon** — grand-orrery convention.
- **Radial-arm construction** matches the standard grand-orrery mechanism (arms driven by clockwork gearing beneath the deck).
- **Tall pedestal silhouette** — Rowley's instrument sat on a floor-standing column, not a table.

## What is interpretation

- **Materials.** Real orrery is brass on a mahogany pedestal; rendered in brass throughout under D-016.
- **Planet sizes are not to scale** — no orrery ever is; if drawn to true relative diameter the inner planets vanish.
- **Orbit radii are proportional but not measured** — chosen to fit the deck and read distinct at gameplay distance, not from Rowley's specific gearing ratios.
- **Fixed pose** — a real orrery is driven; this holds the planets in plausible positions.
- **Clockwork gearing under the deck** is not modelled — hidden inside the pedestal cap.
