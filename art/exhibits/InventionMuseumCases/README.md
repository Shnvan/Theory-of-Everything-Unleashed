## Composition

Four small display objects on a shared plinth, arranged left-to-right:

1. **Gutenberg-type press (c. 1440s)** — wooden screw press with two vertical columns, top and bottom platens, bed, central screw, and horizontal handle.
2. **Pascaline (1642)** — Blaise Pascal's mechanical calculator. Small rectangular box with six number dials on top and a display window strip.
3. **Faraday disk (1831)** — homopolar generator. Vertical disk on an axle between two magnet-pole rectangles.
4. **1903 Wright Flyer** — biplane silhouette: upper and lower wings, four interplane struts, a tail assembly, a forward canard, and a landing skid frame.

## What is accurate

- **Four canonical inventions** — the accuracy doc's specific list.

## What is interpretation

- **Compression: all four are shown at similar display sizes** despite real scale differences. Real Gutenberg press ~2 m tall; Pascaline ~0.3 m wide; Faraday disk ~0.3 m across; Wright Flyer ~12 m wingspan. Factual card must state real dimensions per object and disclose the compression.
- **Materials.** Real Gutenberg is wood; Pascaline is wood + brass; Faraday is brass + iron magnets + copper disk; Wright Flyer is spruce + muslin fabric + wire. Brass throughout under D-016.
- **Simplified silhouettes:**
  - Gutenberg press: no tympan, no frisket, no type case.
  - Pascaline: no visible carry indicators, no engraving.
  - Faraday disk: no brush contacts, no leads.
  - Wright Flyer: no propellers, no engine, no rudder detail.
