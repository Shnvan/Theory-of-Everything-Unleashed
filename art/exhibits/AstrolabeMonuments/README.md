## Composition

**Front and reverse displayed side by side**, per the accuracy doc's requirement.

- **Front disk**: mater with an openwork rete (three crossed arms + zodiac ring) at the standard 17th-century construction. Central pin, throne, and suspension ring at top.
- **Back disk**: mater with alidade (angled sighting rule) and two sight vanes at its ends. Central pin, throne, and suspension ring at top.
- **Shared stand**: base plate with two support columns, one under each disk.

## What is accurate

- **Both faces displayed** — the accuracy doc explicitly requires "front and reverse displayed".
- **Rete/alidade construction** matches every planispheric astrolabe of the type. Openwork rete carrying the zodiac ring on the front; sighting rule alidade on the back.
- **Throne + suspension ring at top of each disk** — the standard hanging configuration.

## What is interpretation

- **Materials.** Real Arsenius astrolabes are engraved brass — the D-016 palette matches this real material exactly, unlike most other exhibits.
- **Mater dimensions.** Arsenius large astrolabes range around 30-40 cm diameter. This uses 14 studs per disk as a display choice; scale not measured against the specific `1878-11` catalogue entry.
- **Rete detail.** Real Arsenius retes carry named star pointers and often 20-30 pointers. Rendered here as three crossed arms + a plain zodiac ring — reads as "an astrolabe rete" without committing to specific star positions.
- **No engraved graticules.** Real mater surfaces carry almucantar circles, azimuth lines, and hour markings. Not modelled — smooth disk surface only.
