## Composition

Two telescopes side by side, **deliberately at different visual scales** to show the 300 years of development between them:

- **Left: Galileo-type refractor** (~1610). Small wooden tube on a splayed tripod, tilted upward. Objective lens flare visible at the sky end.
- **Right: Mount Wilson Hooker Telescope** (1917). Concrete pier, tilted polar axis housing, fork mount with two arms, large Cassegrain tube pointing upward, top ring with spider vanes for the secondary mirror.

## What is accurate

- **Galilean refractor: single-tube refractor on a light tripod** — Galileo's original design; not on any equatorial mount, because equatorial mounts didn't exist yet.
- **Hooker: fork/yoke mount** (simplified from the real English-yoke geometry), **tilted polar axis** at approximately Mount Wilson's latitude of 34°, **large Cassegrain tube** with an obvious top ring for the secondary mirror. These are the silhouette-defining features that identify the object as Hooker rather than any other early-20th-century reflector.
- **Two-orders-of-magnitude size difference** — real Galilean was ~1 m long, real Hooker is ~10 m tube on a 30+ m mount. The side-by-side juxtaposition is the point.

## What is interpretation

- **Neither is at 1:1.** The Galilean is enlarged relative to Hooker so it's still visible; the Hooker is shrunk to fit the envelope. A factual card must disclose that both are compressed and disclose the real dimensions.
- **Materials.** Real Galilean tubes are wood and leather; real Hooker is painted steel. Both rendered in brass under D-016.
- **Hooker mount simplified.** The real object is on an **English-yoke** mount (a specific horseshoe/yoke geometry unique to Hooker); rendered here as a simpler fork mount that reads as "equatorial fork" without committing to Hooker's exact yoke.
- **No dome around Hooker.** The real telescope lives inside a 100-foot dome; not rendered here.
